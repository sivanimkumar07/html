details about the news

The 2018 Kerala floods were a catastrophic natural disaster caused by unusually high monsoon rainfall—42% above normal—between June and August, marking the state's worst deluge in nearly a century. All 14 districts were severely affected, triggering widespread landslides, forcing the opening of 35 dams, and causing over 483 deaths. With 1 in 6 people impacted and massive infrastructural damage estimated at ₹40,000 crore, the crisis showcased immense community resilience alongside significant emergency management challenges.
